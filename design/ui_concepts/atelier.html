Atelier

The interface as a finished dial. Atelier borrows from the quiet end of fine watchmaking: Lange’s gilt-on-black restraint, the engraved caseback, the chapter ring that gives a dial its structure, the jewel that marks a moving part. On a round bedside clock those are not ornaments but the natural grammar. The rim carries the values (brightness, schedule, minutes) the way a bezel carries a scale; pushers at ten and two hold navigation; every knob that moves is a gilt bead with a ruby cabochon, so a sleepy hand knows at once what can be turned. Surfaces are a warm midnight ink instead of black, text is ivory and champagne rather than white, and accents are gilt hairlines, so the room stays dark and the glass reads as an object, not a screen.

Every token is RGB565-exact, every size is a fixed bitmap size, and every texture is a static image. Nothing relies on blur, masks or live transforms.

Colour

Warm midnight surfaces, gilt accents, a ruby jewel. Values are exact in RGB565 (R and B in steps of 8, G in steps of 4), so nothing shifts on the panel. On encre, ivoire reaches 15.8 : 1, champagne 13.7 : 1, gilt 10.2 : 1 and parchment 7.1 : 1, so all text stays readable at 10 % backlight. Taupe (3.1 : 1) is kept for inactive marks that never carry meaning alone.

Type

Cormorant Garamond for titles and numerals, Jost for everything read in passing. Both cover Latin Extended and Cyrillic. Nine bitmap sizes in total; the two display sizes are subset to digits and colon.

Form

Four radii and an 8-point spacing scale. Touch targets are 72 px or larger; rows are 76–112 px.

Icons

24-unit grid, drawn at 28 px with a 1.75 px round stroke. Line only; state is shown by the surface, never by a filled glyph.

Controls

At true device size. The jewel (gilt bead, ruby cabochon) marks everything that moves.
