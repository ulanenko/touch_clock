Touch Clock · 720 × 720 round · UI direction

Instrument.

Treat the clock as a precision instrument, not a phone. The direction borrows from Dieter Rams' Braun clocks and radios, Teenage Engineering's labelled hardware and the engraved scales on a Leica lens: matte warm graphite, 1 px hairlines, monospaced legends printed like panel markings, and one signal orange that only ever means "on", "now" or "act". It suits a bedside round display because the rim becomes a scale you can read and drag. Controls look and behave like keys and slide switches, so their state reads at a glance in the dark and a sleepy thumb can hit them. Nothing glows except what matters.

Building it in LVGL 9.3

  • Keys: lv_button radius 10, 1 px border, the 3 px skirt is a second rect 3 px lower (no live shadow).
  • Switch: plain lv_obj track + thumb object moved with an x animation; the orange bar and off-ring are two small children toggled by state.
  • Rim scales (brightness, hold-to-stop, 24 h dial): lv_arc for track and fill; tick rings are static pre-rendered PNGs (or lv_scale in round mode).
  • Roller: lv_roller, 3 visible rows, selected-row style 88 px, normal rows 34 px ink-3.
  • Dimmed backgrounds: face image at opa 28 % over black; dialogs over a flat 72 % black rect.

Rules kept

  • No blur, no blend modes, no group opacity, no masks: only solid fills, 1 px borders, arcs, lines and static PNGs.
  • One soft shadow, on the dialog only. Gradients: none on device.
  • Fonts at fixed sizes: Inter Tight 22 / 24 / 28 / 34 / 56 / 88 / 144, IBM Plex Mono 18 / 22.
  • Every tappable thing is ≥ 72 px or sits in a ≥ 72 px row.
